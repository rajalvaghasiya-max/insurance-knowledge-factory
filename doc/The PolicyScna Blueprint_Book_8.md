# Book VIII — Engineering Standards

> *"Consistency is the foundation upon which scalability is built."*

---

# Introduction

The Knowledge Factory is expected to evolve for many years.

New contributors will join.

New technologies will emerge.

Artificial Intelligence will continue to improve.

Without shared engineering standards, the factory would gradually become inconsistent, difficult to understand and increasingly expensive to maintain.

Engineering Standards exist to protect the long-term health of the Knowledge Factory.

They provide consistency without limiting innovation.

Every future implementation should follow these standards unless there is a compelling architectural reason not to.

---

# Standard 1 — Responsibilities Before Implementation

Every capability begins with a clearly defined responsibility.

Technology is selected only after responsibilities are understood.

Responsibilities remain stable.

Implementations evolve.

---

# Standard 2 — Asset-First Design

Every new capability begins by answering one question.

> **What Knowledge Asset are we manufacturing?**

Only after the asset has been defined do we design the Engine responsible for manufacturing it.

Assets define Engines.

Engines never define Assets.

---

# Standard 3 — Contract-First Development

Every department communicates through stable contracts.

Implementations remain independent.

Changes to contracts require architectural review.

Stable contracts allow the factory to evolve without disrupting neighbouring departments.

---

# Standard 4 — One Responsibility Per Engine

Every Engine performs one specialised transformation.

Complex workflows emerge from collaboration between Engines rather than by expanding the responsibilities of a single Engine.

---

# Standard 5 — Version Everything

Every meaningful artifact is versioned.

This includes:

Evidence

Knowledge Assets

Contracts

Blueprints

Ontologies

Recommendations

Engineering Standards

History is preserved.

Learning compounds.

---

# Standard 6 — Explainability by Default

Every manufactured asset should retain sufficient information to explain:

Where it originated.

How it was produced.

Why it exists.

Which evidence supports it.

Explainability is part of the architecture.

Not an optional feature.

---

# Standard 7 — Design for Evolution

Every component should be replaceable without redesigning the factory.

Artificial Intelligence models may change.

Programming languages may change.

Responsibilities remain.

---

# Standard 8 — Measure What Matters

Engineering success is measured by outcomes rather than activity.

Examples include:

Increase in understanding.

Evidence coverage.

Knowledge quality.

Explainability.

Reusability.

Maintainability.

The purpose of engineering is not writing code.

The purpose is strengthening the factory.

---

# Standard 9 — Simplicity Before Cleverness

Simple systems are easier to understand, test, explain and improve.

Whenever two designs achieve the same purpose, the simpler design is preferred.

Complexity must always justify its existence.

---

# Standard 10 — Stewardship

Every contributor inherits the responsibility of improving the Knowledge Factory.

No contribution should weaken the long-term health of the platform.

Every generation should leave clearer architecture, better documentation and stronger engineering practices for the generation that follows.

---

# Engineering Review Checklist

Before approving any significant architectural change, every contributor should be able to answer the following questions.

1. Does this increase understanding?
2. Does this strengthen the Knowledge Factory?
3. What Knowledge Asset does this manufacture?
4. Which Department owns it?
5. Which Engine performs the work?
6. Which contract defines it?
7. Which evidence supports it?
8. Is it explainable?
9. Will it still make sense with 100 insurers, 5,000 products and 100,000 documents?
10. Will the next generation thank us for this decision?

If any answer is unclear, the design should be improved before implementation begins.

---

# The Purpose of Standards

Engineering Standards do not exist to restrict creativity.

They exist to preserve clarity.

They ensure that hundreds of future engineering decisions continue moving in the same direction.

Consistency creates predictability.

Predictability creates quality.

Quality creates trust.

Trust increases understanding.

That is why Engineering Standards exist.
